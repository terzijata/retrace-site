Retrace vs Session Buddy

A modern, private session manager for Chrome and Edge.

Short answer: Session Buddy is a long-standing session manager: it saves and restores windows and lets you search them. Retrace does that too, and adds organization and recovery features on top — in a current Material 3 design, with everything kept on your device.

What both do

  • Save windows as sessions and restore them later.
  • Search across saved tabs.
  • Keep your data in the browser, with no account needed.

Where Retrace goes further

  • Spaces — folders for a trip or a project that learn what they're about and suggest (or file) new sessions.
  • Sessions that describe themselves from what the tabs are, instead of a list of site names.
  • Live sessions that follow their window as you open and close tabs.
  • A recovery timeline to slide back through the windows you had open, or search for a page you closed.
  • A Windows view of every open tab, with duplicate cleanup and tab sleeping.
  • Side panel and full page, notes, tags, reminders and optional on-device AI.

Switching from Session Buddy

  1. In Session Buddy, export your collections as JSON.
  2. In Retrace, open Settings › Recovery & data › Import from another tab manager and choose the file.
  3. Each collection or window becomes a session; anything already saved is skipped.

Private by design

Retrace stores everything in your browser, on your device. There's no account and there are no Retrace servers. The extension's security policy blocks network requests outright, so this is enforced by the browser rather than only promised. Optional AI features use the browser's built-in, on-device model.
