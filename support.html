The basics: sessions & Spaces

A session is a saved snapshot of a window's tabs that you can search and reopen later. A Space is a folder that gathers related sessions over time. Retrace has three surfaces that share one design — a compact popup, a side panel, and a full page — and you can work from any of them.

Using the free features

  • Save a window — click Save to store the current window as a session, or press the keyboard shortcut (Ctrl/Cmd+Shift+S). Saving the same window again refreshes that session instead of creating a duplicate.
  • Save just some tabs — in the side panel or full page, tick the tabs you want and the button switches to “Save N tabs.”
  • Tab groups are kept — if the window uses Chrome tab groups, Retrace stores their names and colors and restores them when you reopen the session.
  • Reopen — open a session in a new window, or add its tabs to the current window from the session's detail panel.
  • Pin — pin the sessions that matter so they reopen when your browser starts (you can turn this on in Settings).
  • Archive — tuck sessions you don't need often out of the main list without deleting them.
  • Rename & delete — rename any session, or delete it; destructive actions show an Undo. Deleted sessions also go to a 30-day trash in Settings, so you can restore them later.
  • Sort & multi-select — sort your list by recent, name, or number of tabs, and use Select to archive, delete, or move several sessions to a Space at once.
  • Grow a session — use Add this window to fold the current tabs into an existing session, or remove a single tab from it.
  • Spaces — create folders and move sessions into them to keep related work together. Retrace learns what each Space is about: when you save a window it suggests the right Space (tap Add), and Spaces › Sort unfiled sessions files the rest in a tap and proposes new Spaces. To have clear matches filed for you, choose Settings › Saving › Spaces for new sessions › File automatically when sure (there's always an Undo).
  • Space rules, look & notes — open a Space and press the palette button to give it an emoji, a colour and auto-file rules (sites like jira.acme.com or github.com/acme, and words). Saves that match go straight in. A Space also has a note, and can open all its sessions at once — each in its own window, or all in one window with a tab group per session.
  • Notes — in a session's details, write a note on the session, or press the note icon on any page to note that page. Notes are searchable.
  • Group tabs — press Group tabs next to “Active now” to sort the current window's loose tabs into browser tab groups by topic. Your pinned tabs and existing groups are left alone, and Undo removes the new groups.
  • Search & history — search every session by title, summary, note, page title or web address (including the words of a search you made). Narrow it with site:github.com, in:Work, is:pinned, or dates like “last week” and “yesterday” — or type a sentence. Restore recently closed windows, and browse a searchable log of what you did (you can clear it in Settings).
  • Save & close — in Settings › After saving a window, choose Close saved tabs and every save also closes those tabs to free up memory (browser-pinned tabs are left alone).
  • Keep a session in sync — in a session's details, turn on Keep in sync with its window. While its window is open, the session follows it: tabs you open, close or move are saved automatically. Reopening the session picks the link up again, even after a browser restart.
  • Windows — the Windows tab shows every open window. Search all open tabs, jump to one, drag a tab onto another window to move it, Save all, Close duplicates (with Undo), or press the moon icon to put background tabs to sleep and free memory. Duplicates and sleep are also next to Active now.
  • Share — in a session's details (or a Space's header), press Share to copy the links (plain or Markdown) or save a Retrace file or a web page of links. Nothing is uploaded. Notes are left out unless you tick Include my notes. To open a Retrace file someone sent you: Settings › Import from another tab manager.
  • Tags — in a session's details, type a tag and press Enter (suggested topics appear underneath). Click a tag on any card, or search #tag, to see everything with it.
  • Reminders — press Remind me in a session's details and pick a time. When it's due, the session waits at the top of your list and the toolbar icon shows a number; opening it marks it done.
  • Recovery timeline — every few minutes Retrace notes which pages are open in each window (never incognito). Open Settings › Recovery timeline and drag the slider back through the day, or search for a page you closed. Press Reopen windows to bring that moment back (after a crash, say), or Save as sessions to keep it.
  • Import from another tab manager — Settings › Import from another tab manager accepts a Retrace file someone shared with you, OneTab's exported text (file or paste), Session Buddy and Toby JSON exports, and browser bookmark files.

Using the on-device AI features

Retrace understands your tabs even without AI — summaries, names, Space suggestions and search all work from your tabs' titles and web addresses. On a supported device, it also uses your browser's built-in, on-device AI (Chrome's Gemini Nano, or Edge's own on-device model) to go further. It can:

  • write a sharper one-line summary, a name and a few topics for a session (in your tabs' language, when the model supports it),
  • show a live summary of the window you're in, under its tab titles,
  • group related tabs, and write a short brief,
  • read a search typed as a sentence and find what you meant,
  • help pick the right Space when the match isn't clear, and write a one-line overview of each Space.

How to use it: it mostly just works — when you save a session, Retrace writes the summary and name automatically, and Ask AI in Spaces › Sort unfiled sessions proposes Spaces. The first time, your browser may need to do a one-time download of the AI model in the background; after that it runs offline.

This runs entirely on your device — nothing about your tabs is ever sent anywhere. It needs a reasonably capable desktop (enough disk and memory/GPU for the model). If your device doesn't support it, everything still works from your tabs' titles and addresses. On-device AI is a free bonus, never something you pay for.

Using Retrace Pro (paid)

Retrace Pro is the one optional paid unlock — a one-time $10, available in the Microsoft Edge version of Retrace. One license key turns on the whole bundle: backup & restore (with encryption and a connected-file option), keyboard shortcuts and a command palette, auto-archive, and templates.

  1. Buy Retrace Pro on our checkout. You'll receive a license key by email that looks like RTRC-….
  2. Open Retrace › Settings › Backup & restore, paste your key, and press Unlock. It's verified offline on your device — Retrace Pro turns on right away.
  3. Backup — Export writes a single file containing your sessions, Spaces, history, and settings. Save it wherever you like — it never leaves your device unless you move it.
  4. Backup — Import reads a file you pick and merges it: it adds what's new, keeps the newer copy of anything edited on both sides, and never wipes this device. Sessions you deleted elsewhere go to the trash, so nothing is lost for 30 days.

Use backup to move your data to another device, or to restore after a reinstall. The Chrome version is free and doesn't include the paid features.

More Retrace Pro features

  • Encrypted backups — tick Encrypt the export with a password before exporting. The file is protected with strong on-device encryption; you'll need that password to restore it, and we never store it, so keep it safe.
  • Connected backup file — under Backup & restore, choose Connect a file and pick a location. Retrace keeps that file in two-way sync whenever it's open: it merges in changes from other devices, then writes the combined result back. Put the file in a Dropbox/Drive/OneDrive folder and your sessions follow you across devices — still with no Retrace servers. It's encrypted by default: use the same password on every device. Sync now pulls the latest immediately. If the browser drops the file's permission, a Reconnect button brings it back.
  • Tidy up — in Settings › Tidy up: your library at a glance, plus one-tap fixes for sessions that mostly overlap (merge), sessions you haven't opened in 90 days (archive), pages saved in many sessions, and duplicate pages. Every action can be undone.
  • A longer recovery timeline — Settings › Recovery timeline history keeps up to 30 days (older moments are thinned to four a day).
  • Auto-archive — under Settings › Auto-archive, turn it on and choose a window (7–90 days). Saved, unpinned sessions you haven't opened in that long are archived automatically. It's reversible — nothing is deleted.
  • Templates — under Settings › Templates, use Save this window to store a reusable set of tabs. Press Open to launch it fresh anytime. Unlike a session, a template is a launcher, not a record.

Using keyboard shortcuts (Retrace Pro)

Once Retrace Pro is unlocked, you can drive the side panel and full page from the keyboard — no mouse needed. Every shortcut is rebindable under Settings › Keyboard shortcuts, and pressing ? anytime opens a cheat sheet.

  • J / K — move the selection down / up your list of sessions.
  • Enter open the selected session's details · O reopen all its tabs.
  • P pin/unpin · A archive/unarchive · R rename · D delete (with undo).
  • / jump to search · 1–4 switch filters (All / Pinned / Archived / History).
  • ⌘/Ctrl-K open the command palette — a fuzzy search over commands and your sessions.
  • Esc step back out · ? show the cheat sheet.

Shortcuts work only inside Retrace's own surfaces, so they add no browser permissions. The two global shortcuts (save the current window, open Retrace) are free for everyone; Settings › Browser shortcuts shows what they're set to, and you can rebind them at edge://extensions/shortcuts (on Edge, saving defaults to Alt+Shift+S, since Ctrl+Shift+S is Edge's Web capture).

Common questions

  • My license key hasn't arrived. Keys are issued by hand, so allow a few hours and check your spam/junk. After 2 business days with nothing, email support@retrace-addon.store.
  • My key won't activate. Paste the whole key, including the RTRC- prefix, into Settings › Backup & restore › Unlock. Still stuck? Email us your Paddle order number (never your key) and we'll help you sort it out.
  • The AI isn't writing summaries. Your device may not support on-device AI, or the model is still downloading. Meanwhile Retrace summarizes from your tabs' titles — that's expected, not a fault.
  • A session keeps getting suggested for the wrong Space. Press ✕ next to the suggestion — it won't be suggested there again. A Space's rules always win, so check them in the Space's palette panel.
  • A reopened session didn't restore what was on a page. A session is a record of the tabs you saved, not a snapshot of unsaved in-page content — browsers can't capture that.
