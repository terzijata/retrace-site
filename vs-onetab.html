Retrace vs OneTab

A private, more organized OneTab alternative for Chrome and Edge.

Short answer: OneTab and Retrace both turn a window full of tabs into a saved list you can reopen later, and both are free. OneTab keeps it to one simple list. Retrace is for when that list has grown into hundreds of groups and you need to find things again.

What both do

  • Save the tabs of a window in one click and close them to free up memory.
  • Reopen a whole group, or a single page.
  • Work without an account.

Where Retrace goes further

  • Sessions that describe themselves. A Google search shows as the words you searched, a video or GitHub repo by its name — no more lists of bare site names.
  • Search that understands you: titles, pages, notes and tags, with site:, in:Travel or “last week”.
  • Spaces that gather related sessions (a trip, a project) and suggest where new ones belong.
  • Crash recovery: a timeline of the windows you had open, taken every few minutes.
  • Sessions that stay in sync with their window, notes, tags, reminders, and tab groups saved with their names and colours.
  • A popup, side panel and full page — keep Retrace open beside your tabs.

When OneTab might suit you better

If all you want is the simplest possible “park my tabs” button and you never search your old tabs, OneTab's single list is hard to beat. Retrace can be just as simple — one click to save — but it shines once you have more than a few saved windows.

Switching from OneTab takes a minute

  1. In OneTab, open Export / Import URLs and copy the text on the left.
  2. Install Retrace, open Settings › Recovery & data › Import from another tab manager.
  3. Paste the text and press Import pasted text. Each OneTab group becomes a Retrace session; anything already saved is skipped.

Private by design

Retrace stores everything in your browser, on your device. There's no account and there are no Retrace servers. The extension's security policy blocks network requests outright, so this is enforced by the browser rather than only promised. Optional AI features use the browser's built-in, on-device model.
