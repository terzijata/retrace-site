Retrace vs Toby and Workona

Organize your tabs without an account, a cloud or a subscription.

Short answer: Toby and Workona organize tabs into collections or workspaces, and they sync through an account in the cloud. Retrace gives you the same kind of organization — Spaces, saved sessions, notes — with no account at all, and nothing leaves your device.

Where Retrace is different

  • No account, no cloud. Your sessions live only in your browser. The extension can't make network requests.
  • No subscription. Retrace is free on Chrome and Edge; on Edge an optional one-time Retrace Pro unlock adds backup and power tools.
  • Spaces that organize themselves: Retrace suggests which Space a new session belongs in, and can file it for you with rules like “anything from jira.yourcompany.com”.
  • Sessions that describe themselves, a recovery timeline, live sessions and a Windows view of every open tab.

When Toby or Workona might suit you better

If you need your tabs synced across several computers automatically, or you share collections with a team, a cloud tool is built for that. Retrace deliberately has no servers; on Edge, Retrace Pro can keep an encrypted backup file in a folder you already sync (such as Dropbox or OneDrive).

Switching from Toby

  1. In Toby, export your collections as JSON.
  2. In Retrace, open Settings › Recovery & data › Import from another tab manager and choose the file.
  3. Each collection becomes a session, ready to sort into Spaces.

From Workona, the simplest route is to open a workspace and save its window in Retrace with one click.

Private by design

Retrace stores everything in your browser, on your device. There's no account and there are no Retrace servers. The extension's security policy blocks network requests outright, so this is enforced by the browser rather than only promised. Optional AI features use the browser's built-in, on-device model.
